Traditional Methods Break at Survey Scale
Method How it works Why it breaks at scale
Visual Inspection
  • Experts manually inspect spectral features
  • Slow and labor intensive
  • Not realistic for survey-scale samples
Cross-Correlation
(e.g., Redrock)
  • Matches spectra to predefined templates
  • Weak on noisy or low-resolution spectra
  • Misses unusual cases outside template diversity
  • With the exponential growth of datasets from facilities such as Roman, Euclid, and JWST, these methods are increasingly impractical for processing millions of spectra efficiently.

Fine-Tuning SpecPT on 3D-HST Grism Data
  • Fine-tuning on HST grism data (Momcheva, I. E., et al. 2016) helps extend SpecPT to current and future slitless instruments such as JWST, Roman, and Euclid
  • 3D-HST G141 grism operates at R~130
  • After a quality cut of SNR >= 2.5, the usable sample contains ~8,000 spectra
  • Applied data augmentation techniques to increase the dataset size ~100,000 spectra

Transfer Learning: Pretrain → Adapt → Specialize
  • Pre-training on DESI learns general spectral representations
  • Fine-tuning adapts these representations to new instrument domains
  • Enables effective learning even with limited target data (~8K usable HST spectra)
  • Data augmentation (redshift-shift + balanced sampling) expands effective sample to ~100K
